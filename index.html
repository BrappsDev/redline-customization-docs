§01

Quotes & Order Numbers

How quotations are numbered, what happens on confirmation, and how revisions work.

Numbering

What it does
  • New quotations number from Q06000 upward.
  • When a quote is confirmed it becomes a sales order and gets its permanent S-number (e.g. S06324). Paperwork generated before confirmation updates itself to the new number where it matters (see Freight Forms).

Revising a quote

How to use it
  • Use Create Revision on a quotation to make a new numbered revision (Q06001-2, -3, …) instead of editing history.
  • On an already-confirmed order, Create Revision makes a fresh duplicate draft to work in — the confirmed order is left untouched until the new version is ready.
§02

Order Confirmation Emails

Who gets emailed, and when

What it does
  • Confirming an order from the back end never emails the customer automatically.
  • When the customer accepts / pays through the portal themselves, the confirmation email does go out automatically — that's what they expect.
How to use it
  • After a back-end confirmation, send the confirmation yourself with the Send Confirmation button on the order when you're ready.
§03

Freight Forms

The freight form replaces the old QuickBooks freight sheet: one place to quote a load with the broker, track his rate, and put the freight charge on the order.

Creating the form

How to use it
  • Open any quotation or order and hit Generate Freight Form — it works from draft on, because freight is usually quoted from just a zip code before the customer commits.
  • The form prefills from the order: ship-to, customer PO, estimated ship date, and one model / serial line per unit.
  • Before the customer has completed onboarding, the contact and zip fields stay blank on purpose — type the zip you're quoting to. Once onboarding verifies the real address, the blanks fill themselves in.
Good to know
  • If the onboarded shipping zip doesn't match the zip the freight was quoted to, the salesperson gets a note on the order — nothing is blocked, it's just a flag to re-check the rate.

Serial numbers

What it does
  • Serials generate as RL + MMYY + order number with -1, -2, … per unit (e.g. RL0726S06324-1).
  • Drag rows to reorder (or delete one) and the -1/-2 endings renumber themselves to match the printed order.
  • A serial you've typed by hand is never touched — it keeps its value, and its row still counts toward the numbering.
  • Confirming the order (Q → S rename) re-points the generated serials to the new order number automatically.
Good to know
  • Regenerate Serials throws away the current lines and rebuilds them from the order — use it if the order lines changed a lot.

The two broker emails

How to use it
  • Request Quote — asks the broker to rate the load: destination zip, dimensions, weight, the unit list, and any Special Instructions. No PDF attached. Works from a draft quote.
  • Request Pickup — tells the broker the load is ready: references his own rate-quote number in the subject and attaches the freight form PDF.
  • Both open an email window first — nothing sends until you click Send.
Good to know
  • When the broker replies, his answer lands directly on the freight form's log — no more digging through inboxes.
  • Paste his rate reference in the Broker Rate Quote field on the sales order (next to Customer PO). It flows to the form and into the pickup email automatically.

Freight charge on the order — one line, ever

How to use it
  • Enter the marked-up amount in Freight Charge, then Add Freight Line puts it on the order.
  • After that the form shows Update Freight Line (re-prices the same line from the current charge) and Remove Freight Line.
  • On a confirmed order, Remove sets the line to zero instead of deleting it (Odoo protects confirmed lines) — Update brings the same line back.

Privacy: the customer never sees the freight form

What it does
  • The customer cannot be notified by anything that happens on a freight form — followers are limited to Redline staff, and email suggestions always point at the broker.
  • The broker only receives the two emails you send him — he doesn't see internal notes either.
§04

Parts Freight (UPS Ground)

Auto-calculated parts shipping

How to use it
  • On a parts order, set Shipping Method (UPS Ground vs Air / Expedited) and tick Oversize / Overweight if it applies, then hit Apply Parts Freight.
  • The button calculates the charge from the order subtotal using the agreed percentage rules and maintains one freight line for it.
Good to know
  • Air / expedited is never auto-calculated — quote the carrier's actual cost and add it manually.
  • Parts-freight charges bill to the Freight UPS Ground product; charges from a freight form bill to Flatbed Freight Cost — so the P&L splits them cleanly.
§05

Drop Shipping & Purchasing

Splitting a confirmed order between the warehouse and vendor drop-shipments, without the warehouse ever shipping something twice.

Distribute to Drop Ship

How to use it
  • On a confirmed order, Distribute to Drop Ship lists every line still planned from the warehouse. Enter how much of each should drop-ship and pick the vendor.
  • Applying creates draft RFQs grouped by vendor (shipping straight to the customer) for purchasing to review — nothing is sent to a vendor automatically.
  • The distributed quantity is removed from the warehouse delivery entirely — no crossed-out leftovers on the ticket. If the whole delivery ends up empty, it disappears.

Purchase Products (to our warehouse)

How to use it
  • Purchase Products raises draft RFQs that deliver to Redline — for restocking parts we'll ship ourselves. The customer delivery is untouched.
  • If you flip such a PO to drop-ship afterward (change its Deliver To to the customer), confirming the PO now cleans the warehouse delivery automatically.

Check Procurement

What it does
  • Check Procurement on the order re-syncs the warehouse delivery with reality, both directions:
  • Cancelled a drop-ship RFQ after distributing? The quantity goes back on the delivery.
  • A live drop-ship PO covers something still sitting on the delivery? It comes off.
  • Every fix is summarized in the order's log; a balanced order just says "nothing to fix".
Good to know
  • If something can't be fixed automatically, the salesperson gets a note telling them exactly what to check by hand.
§06

Deliveries & Packing Slips

What the warehouse sees

What it does
  • Whatever the salesperson types under a generic part number on the order line (e.g. the description under an SP302 one-off) now prints on the delivery order — the warehouse knows what the item actually is.
  • The packing slip shows the customer PO, prints partner names cleanly (no "Parent, Child" chains), and shows an Attention: line above the street address when one is set on the contact.
Good to know
  • The duplicate title / doubled footer that used to appear on the packing slip is fixed.
  • The Print button on a delivery prints the delivery slip (customer-facing), not the internal operations list.
§07

Products

The right description in the right app

What it does
  • Sales → Products shows the Sales Description column — the customer-facing wording.
  • Purchase → Products shows the Purchase Description — the vendor's wording that purchasers work with.
Good to know
  • Both columns can be hidden/shown from the column picker (the little ⚙ at the top-right of the list).

Freight products

What it does
  • Three freight service products keep the P&L separated: Flatbed Freight Cost (conveyors / broker rate), LTL Freight (palletized larger parts, manual line), Freight UPS Ground (parts).
  • The system finds them by their internal reference — don't change a freight product's reference without checking with Brapps.
  • Renaming a product's description rewrites how it displays on past orders too — rename with care.
§08

Customer Onboarding

The portal steps

What it does
  • New customers confirm their company, shipping and invoicing details through the portal before an order proceeds — so addresses come from the customer, not from guesswork.
  • Completing the shipping step feeds the verified address into open freight forms automatically (see §03).
Good to know
  • Onboarding never blocks a payment that's already in motion — mismatches notify the salesperson instead.
§09

Payments

ACH / bank transfer — now instant

What it does
  • Customers paying by ACH verify their bank account by logging into their bank — verification is instant.
  • The old micro-deposit path (1–2 business days of waiting, then the customer had to come back) is gone.
Good to know
  • A customer whose bank can't be linked online can still pay by card, wire, or check.
  • Once a customer's account is verified it can be saved for next time — repeat orders skip verification entirely.

Wire & check

What it does
  • Wire instructions live behind a PIN-protected page the customer unlocks — instructions aren't sitting in plain emails.
  • Wire and check payments create a confirmation task for accounting to mark when the money lands; orders that must be paid before production or before shipping are held by their payment terms until then.

Card fees

What it does
  • Credit-card payments add the card processing fee to the amount, so the charge covers itself. The order total the customer approved stays the order total.
§10

Notifications

Who gets accounting alerts

How to use it
  • Payment / wire / check notifications go to the person set in Settings → search "accounting" → accounting notifications field.
  • Covering for someone? Change that one field to yourself and the alerts follow — change it back when they return.
Good to know
  • Everything else only notifies you if you're the salesperson on the record or a follower of it.
  • To check what's actually been paid on an order list, use the Amount Paid column — it reflects real payment transactions, not a manual checkbox.
