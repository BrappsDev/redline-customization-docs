§01

Quotes & Order Numbers

How quotations are numbered, what happens on confirmation, and how revisions work.

Numbering

What it does
  • New quotations number from Q06000 upward.
  • When a quote is confirmed it becomes a sales order and gets its permanent S-number (e.g. S06324). Paperwork generated before confirmation updates itself to the new number where it matters (see Freight Forms).

Revising a quote

How to use it
  • Use Create Revision on a quotation to make a new numbered revision (Q06001-2, -3, …) instead of editing history.
  • On an already-confirmed order, Create Revision makes a fresh duplicate draft to work in — the confirmed order is left untouched until the new version is ready.
§02

Customer Categories

Every customer carries a category (NC / RC / DC codes) that drives their discount, which payment terms they can be offered, and which steps their orders require.

What the category controls

What it does
  • The quote shows a one-line summary under Customer Category — discount, lead terms, and any required steps (credit app, manager approval, signature).
  • The category's discount applies to every order line automatically, including lines added later.
  • The category's allowed payment terms become the only terms selectable on the order.
  • Required steps show as badges under Other Info → Required Steps on the order.

How customers move through categories

What it does
  • A brand-new customer with no order history defaults to NC1 (pay-on-order) when you quote them.
  • When their first order confirms, new-customer categories auto-promote to the matching returning-customer category — you'll see a note on the contact's log.
  • Contacts show a status badge: New (no quotes yet), Shopper (quoted, never ordered), Customer (has ordered).
How to use it
  • Set a customer's category on their contact card, Redline tab. Setting it there also copies the category's payment terms onto the customer.
  • Managers maintain the categories themselves at Sales → Orders → Customer Categories.
§03

Credit Applications

An online application the customer completes from an emailed link. Approval unlocks the requested payment term on their account.

Sending one

How to use it
  • On a quote whose category requires it, hit Credit Application — it creates the application prefilled from the customer.
  • Set Requested Terms (the payment term they're applying for), then Send to Customer — an email window opens with their personal link; nothing sends until you click Send.
  • Applications also live at Sales → Orders → Credit Applications, and the order gets a Credit Apps counter button.

What the customer fills out

What it does
  • The portal page "Application for Credit" asks for business contact info, bank and credit info, three trade references, optional supporting documents, and a typed signature accepting the terms and conditions.
  • Only company name, signature, and the agreement checkbox are strictly required — they can leave the rest blank.
  • When they submit, the salesperson on the order gets notified and the application moves to Submitted.

Approving or declining

How to use it
  • A Sales Manager reviews the submitted application and clicks Approve or Decline (with an optional reason).
  • Approving adds the Requested Terms to the customer's allowed payment terms — their future quotes can offer it immediately.
Good to know
  • The customer is not emailed automatically on approval or decline — let them know yourself.
  • Set Requested Terms before approving; approval without it doesn't unlock anything.
§04

Order Confirmation Emails

Who gets emailed, and when

What it does
  • Confirming an order from the back end never emails the customer automatically.
  • When the customer accepts / pays through the portal themselves, the confirmation email does go out automatically — that's what they expect.
How to use it
  • After a back-end confirmation, send the confirmation yourself with the Send Confirmation button on the order when you're ready.
§05

Freight Forms

The freight form replaces the old QuickBooks freight sheet: one place to quote a load with the broker, track his rate, and put the freight charge on the order.

Creating the form

How to use it
  • Open any quotation or order and hit Generate Freight Form — it works from draft on, because freight is usually quoted from just a zip code before the customer commits.
  • The form prefills from the order: ship-to, customer PO, estimated ship date, and one model / serial line per unit.
  • Before the customer has completed onboarding, the contact and zip fields stay blank on purpose — type the zip you're quoting to. Once onboarding verifies the real address, the blanks fill themselves in.
Good to know
  • If the onboarded shipping zip doesn't match the zip the freight was quoted to, the salesperson gets a note on the order — nothing is blocked, it's just a flag to re-check the rate.

Serial numbers

What it does
  • Serials generate as RL + MMYY + order number with -1, -2, … per unit (e.g. RL0726S06324-1).
  • Drag rows to reorder (or delete one) and the -1/-2 endings renumber themselves to match the printed order.
  • A serial you've typed by hand is never touched — it keeps its value, and its row still counts toward the numbering.
  • Confirming the order (Q → S rename) re-points the generated serials to the new order number automatically.
Good to know
  • Regenerate Serials throws away the current lines and rebuilds them from the order — use it if the order lines changed a lot.

The two broker emails

How to use it
  • Request Quote — asks the broker to rate the load: destination zip, dimensions, weight, the unit list, and any Special Instructions. No PDF attached. Works from a draft quote.
  • Request Pickup — tells the broker the load is ready: references his own rate-quote number in the subject and attaches the freight form PDF.
  • Both open an email window first — nothing sends until you click Send.
Good to know
  • When the broker replies, his answer lands directly on the freight form's log — no more digging through inboxes.
  • Paste his rate reference in the Broker Rate Quote field on the sales order (next to Customer PO). It flows to the form and into the pickup email automatically.

Freight charge on the order — one line, ever

How to use it
  • Enter the marked-up amount in Freight Charge, then Add Freight Line puts it on the order.
  • After that the form shows Update Freight Line (re-prices the same line from the current charge) and Remove Freight Line.
  • On a confirmed order, Remove sets the line to zero instead of deleting it (Odoo protects confirmed lines) — Update brings the same line back.

Privacy: the customer never sees the freight form

What it does
  • The customer cannot be notified by anything that happens on a freight form — followers are limited to Redline staff, and email suggestions always point at the broker.
  • The broker only receives the two emails you send him — he doesn't see internal notes either.
§06

Parts Freight (UPS Ground)

Auto-calculated parts shipping

How to use it
  • On a parts order, set Shipping Method (UPS Ground vs Air / Expedited) and tick Oversize / Overweight if it applies, then hit Apply Parts Freight.
  • The button calculates the charge from the order subtotal using the agreed percentage rules and maintains one freight line for it.
Good to know
  • Air / expedited is never auto-calculated — quote the carrier's actual cost and add it manually.
  • Parts-freight charges bill to the Freight UPS Ground product; charges from a freight form bill to Flatbed Freight Cost — so the P&L splits them cleanly.
§07

Drop Shipping & Purchasing

Splitting a confirmed order between the warehouse and vendor drop-shipments, without the warehouse ever shipping something twice.

Distribute to Drop Ship

How to use it
  • On a confirmed order, Distribute to Drop Ship lists every line still planned from the warehouse. Enter how much of each should drop-ship and pick the vendor.
  • Applying creates draft RFQs grouped by vendor (shipping straight to the customer) for purchasing to review — nothing is sent to a vendor automatically.
  • The distributed quantity is removed from the warehouse delivery entirely — no crossed-out leftovers on the ticket. If the whole delivery ends up empty, it disappears.

Purchase Products (to our warehouse)

How to use it
  • Purchase Products raises draft RFQs that deliver to Redline — for restocking parts we'll ship ourselves. The customer delivery is untouched.
  • If you flip such a PO to drop-ship afterward (change its Deliver To to the customer), confirming the PO now cleans the warehouse delivery automatically.

Check Procurement

What it does
  • Check Procurement on the order re-syncs the warehouse delivery with reality, both directions:
  • Cancelled a drop-ship RFQ after distributing? The quantity goes back on the delivery.
  • A live drop-ship PO covers something still sitting on the delivery? It comes off.
  • Every fix is summarized in the order's log; a balanced order just says "nothing to fix".
Good to know
  • If something can't be fixed automatically, the salesperson gets a note telling them exactly what to check by hand.

Editing a confirmed purchase order

What it does
  • The Product on a purchase order line stays editable after the PO is confirmed — call the vendor, add the extra item to the same PO, done.
  • It stays locked on cancelled POs, POs waiting for approval, and down-payment lines.
§08

Deliveries & Packing Slips

What the warehouse sees

What it does
  • Whatever the salesperson types under a generic part number on the order line (e.g. the description under an SP302 one-off) now prints on the delivery order — the warehouse knows what the item actually is.
  • The packing slip shows the customer PO, prints partner names cleanly (no "Parent, Child" chains), and shows an Attention: line above the street address when one is set on the contact.
Good to know
  • The duplicate title / doubled footer that used to appear on the packing slip is fixed.
  • The Print button on a delivery prints the delivery slip (customer-facing), not the internal operations list.
§09

Products

The right description in the right app

What it does
  • Sales → Products shows the Sales Description column — the customer-facing wording.
  • Purchase → Products shows the Purchase Description — the vendor's wording that purchasers work with.
Good to know
  • Both columns can be hidden/shown from the column picker (the little ⚙ at the top-right of the list).

Freight products

What it does
  • Three freight service products keep the P&L separated: Flatbed Freight Cost (conveyors / broker rate), LTL Freight (palletized larger parts, manual line), Freight UPS Ground (parts).
  • The system finds them by their internal reference — don't change a freight product's reference without checking with Brapps.
  • Renaming a product's description rewrites how it displays on past orders too — rename with care.
§10

Customer Onboarding

A one-minute, three-step portal form — Company, Shipping, Invoicing — so addresses come from the customer, not from guesswork.

The three steps

What it does
  • 1. Company — where the company of record is located.
  • 2. Shipping — pick an address on file or add a new one, with a ship-to name, optional Attention line, and a shipping contact phone.
  • 3. Invoicing — the invoicing contact's email and AP phone, plus optional special invoicing instructions ("mail a paper copy", "upload to our AP portal") that land on the order for accounting to see.
  • Returning customers with everything already on file just review and confirm — the form tells them "Everything we need is already on file."

How it gates the order

What it does
  • An order cannot be confirmed — by us or by the customer paying — until onboarding is complete. The Confirm button stays hidden and the portal nudges the customer to finish first.
  • The order form shows a warning banner while details are missing; the customer sees a matching "Complete your order details" banner on their portal page.
  • Completing the shipping step feeds the verified address into open freight forms automatically (see §05) — a zip mismatch notifies the salesperson without blocking anything.
How to use it
  • Send Onboarding emails the customer their link — available any time, so you can re-send it whenever.
  • Open Onboarding opens the same form for you to fill in live while on the phone with the customer.
§11

Payment Terms & Holds

Which terms a customer can be offered, what prepayment terms actually hold, and how to override a hold when the business calls for it.

Who can be offered what

What it does
  • Each customer has Allowed Payment Terms on their contact (visible to accounting users). Empty means the company default for new customers.
  • Each quote shows Available Payment Terms — the subset offered on this order. The Payment Terms field only accepts terms from that list, and the customer's category feeds it (§02).
  • The first term a customer accepts on a confirmed order locks onto their account — after that, changing their terms takes a Payment Override Manager.

Prepay and pre-shipment holds

What it does
  • Terms like Prepay 100%, Prepay 50 / 50 and Prepay 20 / 40 / 40 — Engineering carry real gates:
  • Prepay portion — the customer must pay it online to confirm the order.
  • Pre-shipment portion — the warehouse cannot validate the delivery while money is still owed. The delivery shows a "Waiting for payment" banner with the exact amount outstanding.
How to use it
  • Customers pick their term right on the portal ("Select your preferred payment term") — changing it logs a note on the order. When they go to pay, a two-step window has them confirm the term first, then choose a payment method.

Overriding a payment hold

How to use it
  • Payment Override Managers see a Other Info → Payment Gate Override group on the order: tick Bypass Payment Gate and enter the required reason.
  • The override records who granted it and when, posts it to the order's log, and lets the delivery validate despite the outstanding balance.
Good to know
  • Only Payment Override Managers can toggle it, and enabling without a reason is refused — every bypass leaves an audit trail.
§12

Paying an Order

The payment window on the portal offers card and ACH, plus wire transfer and — where the terms allow — check.

ACH / bank transfer — now instant

What it does
  • Customers paying by ACH verify their bank account by logging into their bank — verification is instant.
  • The old micro-deposit path (1–2 business days of waiting, then the customer had to come back) is gone.
Good to know
  • A customer whose bank can't be linked online can still pay by card, wire, or check.
  • Once a customer's account is verified it can be saved for next time — repeat orders skip verification entirely.

Wire transfer

What it does
  • Choosing Wire Transfer in the payment window routes the customer to PIN-protected wire instructions — bank details never sit in a plain email.
  • The order then shows "Awaiting receipt of your wire transfer" to the customer and the same banner internally, and the salesperson plus accounting each get a to-do to confirm the wire arrived before payment is registered.

Check

What it does
  • On orders with a prepayment term, the payment window also offers Check. The customer sees the mailing address, the order reference to write on the check, and enters the amount they're mailing.
  • Clicking I'll Mail the Check marks the order as awaiting the check, emails the customer the mailing instructions, and creates a to-do for the salesperson and accounting to confirm when it arrives and clears.
Good to know
  • The order is not confirmed by the promise — it confirms when the check clears and the payment is registered.
  • The check mailing address lives in Settings → Redline Payment Confirmation.

Card fees

What it does
  • Credit-card payments add the card processing fee to the amount, so the charge covers itself. The order total the customer approved stays the order total.
§13

Invoicing & Down Payments

Money collected up front books as a down payment; revenue is invoiced when the work ships.

What happens when a prepaid order confirms

What it does
  • Confirming an order with a prepayment term creates a down-payment invoice automatically: for the amount actually captured (card/ACH), or for the term's prepay percentage when the payment is coming by wire or check.
  • Down payments book to the prepaid-orders account, not revenue — the P&L doesn't recognize the sale before the work is delivered.

Invoicing the work as it ships

How to use it
  • Create Stage Invoice on the confirmed order raises the real invoice at fulfillment (or a mid-stage percentage on engineering-terms orders). The wizard previews Will Be Invoiced before you commit.
  • The stage invoice draws down the posted down payment — the customer only ever owes the remainder.
Good to know
  • Invoice emails to customers come from Accounting <accounting@redlinesystems.com> with the Accounting signature, regardless of who clicks send.
§14

Notifications

Who gets accounting alerts

How to use it
  • Payment / wire / check notifications go to the person set in Settings → search "accounting" → accounting notifications field.
  • Covering for someone? Change that one field to yourself and the alerts follow — change it back when they return.
Good to know
  • Everything else only notifies you if you're the salesperson on the record or a follower of it.
  • To check what's actually been paid on an order list, use the Amount Paid column — it reflects real payment transactions, not a manual checkbox.
